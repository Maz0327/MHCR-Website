Bathroom remodel timeline: each step, in order

Updated October 2026

How long a bathroom takes depends on the room and how much of it changes. A new vanity and a down-to-the-studs remodel are different jobs. Once we've seen yours, we can tell you what to expect. The work goes in the order below, and the same few things tend to hold it up.

The steps

  1. Choose and order. Pick the tile, vanity, fixtures and glass before anything comes out, and get them ordered. Work stalls when a part isn't there on the day it's needed.
  2. Demolition. We take out what's being replaced. This is when hidden problems show up, like water damage or old pipes.
  3. Plumbing and wiring. We move or replace pipes and wiring while the walls are open. If the job has a permit, the inspector checks this work before the walls are closed.
  4. The shower base and waterproofing. A custom shower is built in layers. We build the curb, then pour and level the mud pan by hand. Next comes the liner, then the waterproofing. No tile goes up until that's done.
  5. Tile. We cut and set the tile one piece at a time, then grout it once the setting material has cured. It's often the longest step.
  6. Fixtures. The vanity, toilet, faucets, lights and fan go in. A glass shower door is measured after the tile is finished and then made to size, so it often comes last.
  7. Final walkthrough. We walk through the finished bathroom with you before we call it done.

What slows a bathroom down

  • Changing your mind after demolition starts.
  • Materials that aren't on hand yet.
  • Damage found behind the walls or under the floor.
  • Waiting for an inspection, when the job has a permit.
  • Custom glass, which can't be measured until the tile is in.

How to keep yours moving

  • Make your choices before demolition.
  • Have the materials on site before the old bathroom comes out.
  • If it's your only bathroom, bring it up at the start so we can plan around it.
  • Be easy to reach. Questions come up during the work, and a quick answer keeps it going.
