Kitchen remodel cost in Denver: what drives the price

Updated October 2026

We don't post kitchen prices. Two kitchens the same size can come out far apart, depending on what stays, what moves and what you pick. These are the things that move the number.

The layout

Keeping the sink, range and fridge where they are costs the least. Moving them means moving plumbing, wiring and sometimes gas lines. Taking out a wall costs more again, and more still if it carries weight.

The cabinets

Cabinets are usually the biggest single part of a kitchen budget, so it's worth knowing your options. Solid cabinet boxes can be kept and painted. Refacing puts new doors and fronts on the old boxes. Replacing them costs the most.

Here's one kitchen of ours as an example. We came out to paint an accent wall. After the sample went up, we suggested a bigger refresh instead of new cabinets. We painted the existing cabinets white and added crown molding on top and puck lights underneath. Then we cut the half wall down to counter height, extended the bar and wrapped its base in stone.

The counters

The material matters, but so does how much of it you need and how many cuts it takes. Extending a bar or adding an island adds square footage.

Appliances and electrical

Reusing appliances that still work saves money. Changing the size or fuel of a range can mean cabinet changes, a new gas line or a new circuit. Under-cabinet lights, new outlets and recessed lights all add wiring.

What's behind the walls

Older houses can hide wiring, plumbing or framing that has to be brought up to date once the walls are open. In homes built before 1978, old paint may contain lead, which changes how demolition has to be done. Nobody can see all of this before the work starts, so keep some money aside for it.

Permits

Work that changes plumbing, wiring, gas lines or structure usually needs a permit from your city or county. Permits bring fees and inspections, and the inspections add time. Painting and cabinet work usually don't need one. Your local building department has the final say.

The finishes you pick

Tile, hardware, faucets and light fixtures each come in a wide price range. You choose them, so this is the part of the budget you control most.

Where you can save

  • Keep the layout.
  • Keep solid cabinets and paint them.
  • Spend on what you touch every day, like the faucet, the counter and the drawers, and save on the rest.
  • Make every decision before work starts. Changes in the middle of a job cost the most.
