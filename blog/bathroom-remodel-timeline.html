How Long Does a Bathroom Remodel Take?

February 10, 2026 10 min read

"How long will my bathroom remodel take?" It's the first question homeowners ask—and for good reason. Understanding realistic timelines helps you plan around the disruption, set expectations with family members, and avoid frustration. In this guide, we break down typical bathroom remodel timelines by project type and explain what factors can extend or shorten your renovation.

Quick Answer: Bathroom Remodel Timelines

  • Powder Room/Half Bath: 1-2 weeks
  • Standard Full Bathroom: 3-4 weeks
  • Master Bathroom: 4-6 weeks
  • Major Renovation (Layout Changes): 6-10 weeks
  • Luxury/Spa Bathroom: 8-12+ weeks

Add 1-3 weeks for material lead times and permit approval in Denver.

Bathroom Remodel Timeline by Type

Powder Room/Half Bath: 1-2 Weeks

Powder rooms are the fastest bathroom remodels since they typically only include a toilet and sink—no shower or tub work.

What's included:

  • New vanity and sink
  • Toilet replacement
  • Flooring update
  • New faucet, mirror, lighting
  • Fresh paint

Timeline breakdown: Demo (half day), plumbing (1 day), flooring (1-2 days), installation (2-3 days), finishing (1-2 days).

Standard Full Bathroom: 3-4 Weeks

A full bathroom remodel (40-100 sq ft) with tub/shower, toilet, and vanity is the most common project type.

What's included:

  • Tub or shower replacement/remodel
  • New vanity with countertop
  • Toilet replacement
  • Tile work (floor and shower surround)
  • New fixtures and accessories
  • Lighting and ventilation updates

Timeline breakdown: Demo (1-2 days), rough plumbing/electrical (2-3 days), cement board/waterproofing (1-2 days), tile (5-7 days), fixture installation (2-3 days), finishing (2-3 days).

Master Bathroom: 4-6 Weeks

Master bathrooms are larger and typically feature more complex elements like dual vanities, walk-in showers, and freestanding tubs.

What's included:

  • Walk-in shower with multiple showerheads
  • Freestanding or built-in tub
  • Double vanity with storage
  • Heated floors
  • Custom tile work
  • Updated electrical for spa features

Timeline breakdown: Demo (2-3 days), rough work (3-5 days), waterproofing/substrate (2-3 days), tile (7-10 days), fixtures (3-5 days), finishing (3-4 days).

Major Renovation with Layout Changes: 6-10 Weeks

Moving plumbing, walls, or expanding the bathroom footprint significantly extends the timeline.

Additional work includes:

  • Wall removal or addition
  • Plumbing relocation
  • Electrical panel upgrades
  • Structural modifications
  • HVAC adjustments

Why it takes longer: Permits require additional inspection phases. Moving drain lines requires cutting into floors. Structural work needs engineering approval. Multiple trades must coordinate more complex schedules.

Bathroom Remodel Phases Explained

Understanding each phase helps you anticipate what's happening and why certain steps can't be rushed.

Phase 1: Planning & Permits (1-3 weeks before work starts)

  • Design finalization
  • Material selection and ordering
  • Permit applications (Denver typically 1-2 weeks)
  • Contractor scheduling

Phase 2: Demolition (1-3 days)

  • Removal of existing fixtures, tile, and finishes
  • Inspection of underlying structure
  • Addressing any hidden issues (water damage, mold, outdated plumbing)

Phase 3: Rough-In Work (2-5 days)

  • Plumbing rough-in for new fixture locations
  • Electrical wiring for lights, fans, outlets
  • Inspection by city inspector

Phase 4: Waterproofing & Substrate (1-3 days)

  • Cement board installation
  • Waterproof membrane application
  • Shower pan installation or preparation
  • Cure time for waterproofing (24+ hours)

Phase 5: Tile Installation (5-10 days)

This is typically the longest phase:

  • Shower/tub surround tile (2-4 days)
  • Floor tile (1-2 days)
  • Grout application and curing (1-2 days)
  • Sealing (1 day with cure time)

Phase 6: Fixture Installation (2-4 days)

  • Vanity and countertop
  • Toilet
  • Shower fixtures and glass
  • Lighting and exhaust fan
  • Accessories (towel bars, toilet paper holder)

Phase 7: Finishing & Final Inspection (2-3 days)

  • Painting and touch-ups
  • Caulking
  • Final cleaning
  • City final inspection
  • Walkthrough with homeowner

Factors That Affect Duration

Material Availability

Custom tile, specialty fixtures, and imported materials can add 2-8 weeks to lead time. Order materials before demolition begins whenever possible.

Permit Processing

Denver permit approval typically takes 1-2 weeks. Complex projects requiring structural review can take 3-4 weeks. Plan ahead and submit permits early.

Hidden Issues

Once demolition begins, contractors may discover:

  • Water damage or mold (adds 2-7 days for remediation)
  • Outdated plumbing (galvanized pipes need replacement)
  • Electrical not to code (upgrade required)
  • Structural issues (adds 1-2 weeks)

Budget tip: Include a 10-15% contingency in your budget and timeline for unknowns.

Project Complexity

Features that extend timeline:

  • Heated floors: +1-2 days
  • Custom tile patterns: +2-4 days
  • Steam shower: +2-3 days
  • Custom glass enclosure: +1-2 weeks (lead time)
  • Custom cabinetry: +2-4 weeks (lead time)

Contractor Schedule

Peak season (spring-fall) means busier contractor schedules. Starting your project in late fall or winter can result in faster completion and more flexible scheduling.

Tips to Stay on Schedule

1. Finalize All Decisions Before Demo

Changing your mind about tile, fixtures, or layout mid-project is the #1 cause of delays. Make all selections before work begins.

2. Order Materials Early

Have all materials on-site or confirmed for delivery before demolition. This prevents work stoppages waiting for backorders.

3. Hire One Contractor to Manage the Project

A general contractor coordinates all trades (plumber, electrician, tile installer) so work flows efficiently without gaps between phases.

4. Plan for Backup Bathroom Access

If this is your only bathroom, discuss timeline compression or temporary accommodations. Some contractors offer expedited schedules for higher fees.

5. Be Available for Decisions

Quick responses to contractor questions prevent delays. Designate one decision-maker who can be reached easily during work hours.

6. Expect the Unexpected

Add a buffer of 1-2 weeks to any timeline for unforeseen issues. It's better to be pleasantly surprised than constantly frustrated.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a full bathroom remodel take?

A full bathroom remodel typically takes 3-4 weeks for a standard bathroom (40-100 sq ft) with no layout changes. This includes demolition (1-2 days), rough plumbing and electrical (2-3 days), waterproofing and tile (5-7 days), fixture installation (2-3 days), and finishing work (2-3 days).

Can a bathroom be remodeled in 2 weeks?

Yes, a small bathroom or powder room can be remodeled in 1-2 weeks if there are no layout changes, materials are pre-ordered, permits aren't required, and work proceeds without delays. However, most full bathroom remodels realistically take 3-4 weeks or longer.

What takes the longest in a bathroom remodel?

Tile work typically takes the longest, accounting for 5-10 days depending on complexity. This includes shower/tub surround tiling, floor tile installation, and grout curing time. Custom tile patterns, heated floors, or large-format tiles can extend this timeline further.

How long does a master bathroom remodel take?

A master bathroom remodel typically takes 4-6 weeks due to larger size and more complex features like dual vanities, walk-in showers, freestanding tubs, and custom cabinetry. Major master bath renovations with layout changes can take 8-12 weeks.
