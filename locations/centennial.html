The Suburban Homes of Centennial

A young city built from mature neighborhoods, each with its own era

Mid-Century Ranches (1960s-1970s)

Cherry Knolls, Southglenn, and Walnut Hills are anchored by ranches on established, tree-lined lots. Their simple structures welcome opened kitchens, updated baths, and finished basements.

1980s and 1990s Two-Stories

Smoky Hill and Piney Creek added formal, compartmentalized two-story homes with oak trim and brass fixtures, ideal for open-concept conversions, ceiling updates, and finish refreshes.

Split-Levels and Tri-Levels

The staggered-floor homes scattered through Centennial's mid-era neighborhoods have layouts many owners want reworked. We connect the levels and finish the lower floors into comfortable space.

Semi-Rural Foxfield Properties

Foxfield's large-lot and horse-friendly homes stand apart from the standard subdivisions. Renovations here use the acreage with expanded kitchens, suite additions, and flexible bonus spaces.

Planning Your Centennial Renovation

Because Centennial's homes largely predate the city itself, many were built to older electrical and structural standards, so modernizing wiring and adding support beams during open-concept work is routine. The City of Centennial administers permitting, and we manage that for you.

The metro's expansive clay soils and radon exposure apply across Centennial, making testing worthwhile before a basement finish. When a remodel needs smaller repairs alongside the main work, our handyman services cover the details, and neighbors to the north can review our Aurora remodeling services.
